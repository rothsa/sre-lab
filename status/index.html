Down or Me

Checks these services every 5 minutes. When something looks wrong, an AI classifier (Jev) decides whether it's a real outage, degraded, planned maintenance, a blip, or a problem on the monitor's side, and how confident it reports being. Verdicts under 80% confidence are marked for a human look.

What changes in the agentic era

  • A classifier, not a chatbot. The checker sends Jev the latest result, recent history and how many other services are failing, and Jev picks one of five fixed causes and says how confident it is. TypeSafe, which makes Jev, describes it as returning typed decisions with calibrated probabilities, in early access. It can't run commands or write free text, so a wrong answer can only mislabel a row or send or skip one email.
  • It alerts when both confidence thresholds are met. An email goes out only when Jev reports at least 80% confidence in the cause and at least an 80% probability that the owner should hear about it, the cause is a real outage or degradation, and no email has gone out for this problem yet. A verdict under 80% confidence also shows on the board as “needs a human look”. That trades a few late or missed emails for fewer false ones, the same precision-versus-recall choice as any alert threshold.
  • Caps keep the AI optional. Jev is asked only when a check looks unusual, at most every 30 minutes while a problem lasts, and under a daily cap (JEV_DAILY_CAP, default 200). Past the cap, failures still show on the board without a verdict: the monitor never needs the model to see an outage.
  • What a human still decides. Status-page incident titles from other companies go into Jev's input, and that is untrusted text; the fixed answer set limits what injected text could do, but it could still nudge a label. Confidence is the model's own estimate, so a person still decides what to do about an outage and whether 80% is the right line, by checking past verdicts against what actually happened.
